Modernize the system that still runs the business — without the big-bang rewrite that so often kills it.

The Mandate

The legacy system is usually the one that actually runs the business — which is exactly why modernizing it is terrifying. The big-bang rewrite is the classic trap: a multi-year project to replace a working system that ends in a worse one, or no system at all.

I modernize safely and incrementally. Understand what the old system truly does, including the undocumented behavior people depend on, then evolve it in controlled steps that each keep the business running. Refactoring and migration without betting the company on a single switch-flip.

Engagement Scope
  • Legacy-system analysis and behavior documentation
  • Incremental modernization and migration strategy
  • High-performance refactoring of critical paths
  • Strangler-pattern and phased-replacement approaches
  • Technical-debt assessment and prioritization
  • Data-migration and integrity strategy
  • Risk mitigation and rollback planning per step
  • Knowledge recovery from undocumented systems
How I Work

I work in controlled increments, each shippable and reversible, so the business keeps running throughout. The hidden behaviors that everyone depends on and no one documented are recovered first, because those are what a careless rewrite destroys. No single step is allowed to be a bet-the-company move.

Every engagement runs under a signed NDA and, where testing is involved, a Safe Harbor agreement. Legal and ethical, start to finish.

What You Receive
  1. A modernization strategy with phased, reversible steps
  2. Recovered documentation of true system behavior
  3. Refactored critical paths with measured improvement
Questions
Should we rewrite or refactor?+

Usually refactor and migrate incrementally. Full rewrites of working systems are high-risk and frequently fail. Incremental modernization keeps the business running while the system evolves.

The original developers are gone — can you still help?+

Yes. Recovering the true, undocumented behavior of a system whose authors have left is a core part of the work and the first step before any safe change.
