The Mandate

Scale breaks the assumptions that work at small size. The database that was instant becomes the bottleneck; the service that never failed now fails constantly because there are a thousand of it. Distributed systems are hard precisely because the failure modes are emergent — they appear only under real load.

I design for that reality from the start: throughput, consistency, and graceful failure as first-class concerns, not afterthoughts bolted on when the pager starts firing. The result is a system that stays fast under pressure and degrades predictably when something inevitably breaks.

Engagement Scope
  • Distributed-system and microservices architecture
  • High-throughput and low-latency system design
  • Data consistency, partitioning, and sharding strategy
  • Service decomposition and boundary design
  • Message queues, streaming, and event-driven patterns
  • Fault tolerance, backpressure, and graceful degradation
  • Observability, tracing, and capacity planning
  • Performance analysis and bottleneck elimination
How I Work

I design around the real load profile and the real failure modes, choosing consistency and partitioning models deliberately rather than by default. Failure is planned for explicitly — backpressure, degradation, and recovery — so the system bends under stress instead of snapping.

Every engagement runs under a signed NDA and, where testing is involved, a Safe Harbor agreement. Legal and ethical, start to finish.
