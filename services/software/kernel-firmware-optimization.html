The Mandate

Close to the hardware, the rules change. There is no garbage collector to save you, the latency budget is in microseconds, and the memory is measured in kilobytes. This is where general-purpose engineers stall and specialists earn their keep.

I do that work: kernel-level code, firmware, device drivers, and embedded systems where every byte and cycle counts. Optimization that squeezes real performance out of constrained hardware, and the low-level engineering most teams would rather not touch.

Engagement Scope
  • Kernel-module and device-driver development
  • Firmware development and optimization
  • Embedded and real-time systems engineering
  • Memory, power, and performance optimization under tight constraints
  • Low-latency and deterministic-timing work
  • Hardware/software interface and bring-up
  • Bootloader and low-level boot-path engineering
  • Profiling and bottleneck elimination at the metal
How I Work

I profile before I optimize and measure after, because at this level intuition is often wrong and only the numbers are honest. Work is done with full respect for the hardware constraints — memory, power, timing — and verified against them rather than against a desktop that lies about real behavior.

Every engagement runs under a signed NDA and, where testing is involved, a Safe Harbor agreement. Legal and ethical, start to finish.

What You Receive
  1. Optimized low-level code meeting hard constraints
  2. Measured before/after performance and resource analysis
  3. Documentation of the hardware interface and assumptions
Questions
What platforms and targets do you cover?+

From kernel modules and drivers on general systems to firmware on constrained microcontrollers and real-time embedded targets. The constraints define the approach.

Can you optimize existing firmware?+

Yes. Profiling an existing system and eliminating the real bottlenecks — memory, power, latency — is as common as greenfield work, and usually higher-leverage.
