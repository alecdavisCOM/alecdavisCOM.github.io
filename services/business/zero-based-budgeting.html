The Mandate

Traditional budgets are last year plus a little. That math quietly funds every dead program and bloated line forever, because no one is asked to defend what already exists. Zero-based budgeting flips the default: nothing is entitled to its budget, everything earns it.

Done well, this is not slash-and-burn — it is redirection. I build the architecture and the cost visibility to move money out of low-value inertia and into the things that actually drive the business, then make it a repeatable discipline rather than a one-time purge.

Engagement Scope
  • Cost-base decomposition into visible, owned categories
  • Cost-driver analysis and value mapping per category
  • Zero-based rebuild of discretionary and overhead spend
  • Cost-category ownership and accountability model
  • Policy and approval-threshold redesign
  • Target-setting and savings-tracking framework
  • Reinvestment prioritization toward value drivers
  • Repeatable ZBB operating cadence and governance
How I Work

I pair hard cost visibility with clear ownership so the savings stick instead of creeping back. The point is a permanent change in how spending decisions get made — a discipline the organization runs itself, not a consultant's one-off cut.

Every engagement runs under a signed NDA and, where testing is involved, a Safe Harbor agreement. Legal and ethical, start to finish.

Questions
Is ZBB just cost-cutting?+

No. It is cost reallocation. The discipline frees money from low-value spend so it can fund what actually drives growth. Pure cutting is a byproduct, not the goal.

Not if ownership and cadence are built in. The architecture assigns every cost category an owner and a recurring review, which is what makes the change durable.
