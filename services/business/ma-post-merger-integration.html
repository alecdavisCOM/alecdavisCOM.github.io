M&A Post-Merger Operational Integration

The Mandate

Most deals are won in diligence and lost in integration. The synergies in the model are real only if two sets of systems, people, and processes actually merge — and that is where acquisitions quietly bleed the premium they paid.

I run integration as the operational program it is: a sequenced plan with owners, a protected core business, and a relentless focus on the synergies that justified the price. Day one works, customers stay, the best people don't walk, and the value survives contact with reality.

Engagement Scope
  • Integration management office stand-up and governance
  • Day-one readiness and the first-100-days operating plan
  • Systems, data, and technology consolidation roadmap
  • Organization design, role mapping, and retention of key talent
  • Synergy identification, tracking, and realization against the deal model
  • Process harmonization across finance, operations, and go-to-market
  • Culture integration and internal-communications strategy
  • Customer and vendor continuity management through the transition
How I Work

I protect the base business while the merge happens underneath it — nothing matters if the core stalls during integration. Synergy targets are tracked like financials, with owners and deadlines, so the number in the board deck becomes the number in the results.

Every engagement runs under a signed NDA and, where testing is involved, a Safe Harbor agreement. Legal and ethical, start to finish.

What You Receive
  1. A day-one readiness and first-100-days integration plan
  2. A synergy-realization tracker tied to the deal model with named owners
  3. A target operating model for the combined organization

Before close, ideally during diligence. Day-one readiness and the first-100-days plan should exist the moment the deal signs, not be assembled afterward.
