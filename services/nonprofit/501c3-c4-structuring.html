501(c)(3) & (c)(4) Dual-Track Organizational Structuring

A charitable arm and an advocacy arm, built side by side — each doing what the other legally can't.

The Mandate

A (c)(3) can take deductible gifts and do charitable work but is sharply limited on politics. A (c)(4) can advocate and lobby freely but can't offer donors a deduction. Many missions need both capabilities — and the answer is a deliberate dual-track structure, not forcing one entity to do a job the law won't let it.

I design that structure: a charitable (c)(3) and an advocacy (c)(4) built to work in concert, with the firewalls, cost-sharing, and governance that keep the relationship clean. Each arm does what it's built for, and the wall between them holds up to scrutiny.

How I Work

The structure is built around strict, well-documented separation — the firewall between charitable and advocacy activity is where these arrangements succeed or get into trouble. I design it for compliance from the start and coordinate with nonprofit counsel on formal filings and opinions.

Every engagement runs under a signed NDA and, where testing is involved, a Safe Harbor agreement. Legal and ethical, start to finish.

Questions
Why not just use one entity?+

Because no single entity can both offer donors a deduction and advocate without limit. A dual-track structure lets each arm do what it's legally built for, connected by a compliant firewall.

Is coordinating a (c)(3) and (c)(4) legal?+

Yes, within rules. The two can share resources and work toward a common mission if separation, cost-sharing, and activity limits are properly structured and documented — which is the core of this work.
