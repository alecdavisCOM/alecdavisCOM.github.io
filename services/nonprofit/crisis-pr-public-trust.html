A nonprofit runs on trust. When that trust is attacked, the response has to protect it — fast and honestly.

The Mandate

For a mission-driven organization, trust is the entire balance sheet. Donors give, volunteers serve, and partners sign on because they believe. A scandal, a credible attack, or a visible failure threatens not just reputation but the willingness of everyone to keep believing — and that can unravel fast.

I build the systems to defend that trust: the crisis response for when something breaks, and the standing infrastructure — transparency, monitoring, rapid response — that makes the organization resilient to attack in the first place. Honest, fast, and built to keep faith intact under fire.

Engagement Scope
  • Crisis-communications planning and response
  • Public-trust and reputation-defense systems
  • Donor- and stakeholder-confidence management
  • Transparency and accountability frameworks
  • Rapid-response and monitoring infrastructure
  • Narrative and messaging strategy under attack
  • Internal and board crisis coordination
  • Reputation recovery and trust rebuilding
How I Work

For nonprofits, honesty is the only strategy that survives — transparency and accountability both prevent crises and resolve them, where spin would destroy the trust the mission runs on. I build the standing systems that make the organization resilient and the response plan for when a crisis comes anyway.

Every engagement runs under a signed NDA and, where testing is involved, a Safe Harbor agreement. Legal and ethical, start to finish.

Questions
How is nonprofit crisis PR different?+

The currency is trust, not sales. Tactics that might work for a company — spin, deflection — destroy the donor and public confidence a mission depends on. Transparency and honest accountability are the only durable approach.

Can you help before a crisis hits?+

Yes, and that's the higher-leverage work. Standing transparency, monitoring, and rapid-response systems make an organization resilient to attack and ready to respond well if one lands.
