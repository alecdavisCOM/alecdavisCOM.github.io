Research

Every day is a day for research. It is the engine under everything else I do — and the reason I tend to see the move before it is made.

The Discipline

I cannot not research. My mind wanders and wonders, and it has done so since the sixth grade. Offensive security is the constant, but the curiosity spills into cryptography, protocols, markets, jurisdictions, artificial intelligence, and the soft human edges where every hard system actually fails.

That breadth is not a distraction. It is the advantage. The same pattern recognition that finds a flaw in a cryptographic handshake finds the flaw in a corporate structure or a campaign's messaging. Research is one muscle, pointed at many targets.

In Practice

In security, research means original work: bug bounties, zero-day discovery, exploit analysis, threat-actor study, and reverse engineering of the things everyone trusts and no one has actually read. When I find something, I prove it — a working proof-of-concept so the risk is undeniable.

And then I disclose it responsibly. Found-here-stays-here. Nothing sold, nothing brokered. The work exists to make you safer, not to be traded.

Disclosure & Ethics

All research is conducted legally and ethically. Where it touches a live system, it happens under a Safe Harbor agreement and an NDA. Where it touches a third party's product, I coordinate disclosure responsibly with that vendor.

If you run a bug-bounty program or need a researcher who will go deeper than a scanner and hand you something real, reach me on an encrypted channel.
